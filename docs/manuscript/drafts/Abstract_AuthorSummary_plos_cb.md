## Abstract

Left and right atria maintain distinct healthy expression programs, so mixing chamber identity can make atrial fibrillation (AF) transcriptomic studies hard to compare across cohorts. We introduce a chamber-aware framework based on a frozen Normal Chamber Prior (NCP) comprising 30 programs organized into 16 families. Applied without redefining gene programs in each dataset, the same map was scored across three paired-chamber bulk cohorts. In three paired-chamber bulk cohorts comprising 25 atrial fibrillation (AF) and 26 sinus-rhythm donors, cross-cohort reproducibility was selective across both chamber-biased arms: six families spanning LA-biased translation and ribosome-quality-control programs and RA-biased matrix, proteoglycan, and developmental-signaling programs were classified as High consistency, whereas eight were Moderate and two Low. In left-atrial single-nucleus analyses, four families were non-Below-detection—F10 Fidelity, F11 Relay, F15 Relay and F16 Extension—under an LA-only, nominal-localization boundary that is not FDR-controlled discovery. A cross-sectional duration layer supplied chronicity context rather than a progression, recurrence or treatment-response model. A predefined rule-based Path A, using exactly three gates (bulk High consistency, snRNA non-Below-detection and duration non-Stable), prioritized F10 (GAG disease / TGF-β) and F15 (NTRK2 / TrkB); genetics and spatial assays were not Path A gates and do not constitute causal validation. Genetic evidence was treated as calibration-limited, non-nested traceability: local AF GWAS support was present for 7/12 anchors, yet no anchor reached PP4 ≥ 0.70, which does not exclude cis-regulatory mechanisms. The contribution is a reusable chamber-aware coordinate system for comparing AF datasets, not a single-mechanism, longitudinal or genetically confirmed causal account.

## Author Summary

We studied how the upper chambers of the heart change in atrial fibrillation, a common irregular heartbeat. The left and right atria are molecularly different, and gene-expression studies become hard to compare when chamber context is ignored or mixed. We built a fixed healthy chamber-aware map and applied the same map across multiple public datasets without redefining the gene programs in each cohort. Some of the most reproducible changes involved the tissue scaffold, proteoglycans, signaling, and selected protein-production programs, whereas many metabolic and mitochondrial changes varied more between cohorts. Cell-level analyses localized a small subset of signals, and two families—not one—received the strongest rule-based priority across all three transcriptomic evidence layers. Duration data provided cross-sectional context rather than a time-course model; spatial data supplied tissue-location context at the region-of-interest level; and genetic data helped trace and calibrate candidates but could not rule mechanisms in or out. Our contribution is a reusable chamber-aware framework for comparing atrial fibrillation datasets.
